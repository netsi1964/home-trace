    Pre-alpha · Phase 0 · Open source · Apache-2.0

    Give your home a visual memory.

    Register your belongings room by room with your phone's camera. Later, hold up a faint copy of the old photo, line it up, and see what you find again, what looks moved and what you could not spot. No account. Your photos stay on your device.

    How it works → What AI can really do
    Motivation

    You know what you own. Until you need to.

    Where did the good scissors end up? Is the lamp in the photos for the insurance still in the living room? Nobody keeps a spreadsheet of their home, and a camera roll does not know that the cup in two photos is the same cup.

    What have I registered?

    An inventory with photos, rooms and the date each item was last seen. Five photos of one lamp count as one lamp.

    Is this the same item?

    The app proposes matches with the old and new crop side by side. You answer Same object? Yes / No / Skip.

    What looks different?

    Revisit a viewpoint with the old photo as a transparent overlay and compare. Proposals, never verdicts.

    Design principles
    AI proposes, you decideIdentity is only committed after your answer. Later AI output never overrides it.
    No false certainty"Not rediscovered" never means "removed". Missing confidence stays empty, never an invented number. We say No change detected, never "has not moved".
    Your data, your deviceNo account, no cloud database. Everything exports to an open ZIP with JSON and images.
    Never colour aloneThe first tester is colour-blind. Every status has a label, an icon and a number.
    The journey Planned app flow

    Scan, decide, revisit, compare.

    One button press is one bounded job. The live camera preview never triggers analysis, and the frame sent for analysis never contains the overlay or any boxes.

    Organisation Planned

    Rooms, and named places to stand.

    A room has several viewpoints, like From the doorway or Bookshelf from the sofa. Each viewpoint keeps its scan history and one reference scan you choose yourself.

    Example
    • Living room
      • From the doorway · 3 scans · reference: 12 Oct
      • Bookshelf from the sofa · 1 scan
    • Kitchen
      • Counter by the window · 2 scans
    Rules that keep data honest
    • A new scan never replaces the reference automatically.
    • One lamp seen from two viewpoints is still one inventory item, with two observations.
    • History keeps the room an item was in at the time, even after it moves.
    • Changing a reference affects future comparisons only.
    Identity Planned

    The one question that matters.

    AI is good at "this looks like a cup". It is bad at "this is your cup and not the identical one next to it". So HomeTrace asks you, one item at a time.

    Same object?

    3 of 12 reviewed

    Before · Living room · 12 Oct

    Now · Kitchen · 19 Oct

    ✓ Yes✕ No→ Skip
    What each answer does
    • Yes links the new observation to the item's stable ID.
    • No rejects this match only. It never creates a new item by itself; you choose another candidate or create one.
    • Skip keeps the observation unresolved. It does not count as an extra item.
    • Merges and corrections can be undone.

    Two identical chairs may stay unresolved forever. That is allowed.

    Revisits Planned

    Stand where you stood last time.

    The reference photo lies over the live camera at adjustable opacity. Line up door frames and walls, then tap Ready — scan. The overlay is never part of the captured frame.

    Reference opacity40 %
    Rules
    • Opacity from 0 to 80 %, with a readable percentage and an accessible slider.
    • The full reference is shown in a frame. No hidden cropping when aspect ratios differ.
    • No claim of 3D positioning. If views cannot be compared, the result says Uncertain viewpoint.
    • Moving the slider sends nothing anywhere.
    Compare Planned

    Careful words for what changed.

    Identity, visibility and movement are separate. A cup hidden behind a book is not rediscovered, not gone. A box that moved in the photo is not proof that the chair moved.

    StatusIconMeaningExample
    Found again — no change detected✓Same item, no change seen in this comparison4 items
    Possibly moved↔?Same item, position looks different; you review it1 item
    Moved — confirmed↔You confirmed a new place0 items
    New to inventory＋You created it now; it may have been there for years2 items
    Not rediscovered in this scan◌Seen before, not linked now. Could be hidden or out of view1 item
    Unresolved?Not enough to decide identity or visibility1 item

    "What has not moved?" is answered as Found again with no change detected, with a note that movement between scans cannot be ruled out.

    Privacy & portability Planned

    No account. No cloud inventory.

    Inventory, photos and decisions live in your browser. A ZIP export with open, versioned JSON and image files is your backup and your way out.

    On the device

    IndexedDB holds metadata, images and drafts. Every decision is saved at once, and nothing is deleted to free space.

    Portable ZIP

    manifest.json, inventory.json and assets/, with SHA-256 hashes. Imports are validated and roll back on failure.

    What leaves the phone

    Only the captured frame and needed crops, and only when you tap Scan. API keys live on the server, never in the app, URLs or exports.

    Browser storage can be cleared. HomeTrace reminds you to export and never calls local storage a backup.

    Phase 0 · measured Built Demo data

    Measure first, promise later.

    Before building the app, HomeTrace tests whether AI can find and recognise household objects. Three strategies run through the same harness against hand-annotated ground truth.

    StrategyHowPrecisionRecallPer image
    JEV (TypeSafe)Count 20 categories, locate each, split the image for repeats94 %89 %0.6–2.7 s
    Vision-LLM (Qwen3-VL)"List every object with a box" in one call100 %100 %8–21 s
    Vision-LLM + JEVThe vision model finds, JEV checks each box and judges identity100 %100 %3–20 s

    ◆ Demo: four synthetic drawings with 19 objects, 20 October 2026. These numbers check that the pipeline works; they say nothing about real homes yet. Gate for real photos: ≥ 12 photos, 90 % precision, 80 % recall.

    Findings so far
    • JEV answers one box per question and has no "not found", so every box is paired with a yes/no check.
    • "The rightmost cup" returned the left cup again. Splitting the image into strips fixed it.
    • Asked "same object?", JEV said yes to 3 of 5 identical-looking pairs. That is why you confirm every match.
    Verified contracts
    • JEV via OpenRouter, from official docs and live responses.
    • Vision-LLM via OpenRouter: Qwen3-VL and Gemini box formats.
    • Hard request budgets, no automatic retries of paid calls, logs without image data.
    For contributors Built

    Annotate your own photos and measure.

    The repo ships an in-browser annotation tool and an evaluation script. Photos never leave your machine until you run a paid evaluation yourself.

    1. Set up

      terminal
      $ git clone https://github.com/netsi1964/home-trace
$ cd home-trace && nvm use && npm install
$ npm run dev

    2. Annotate

      Open /phase0/annotate, choose photos, drag boxes, give the same key to the same item in different photos. Export groundtruth.json.

    1. Free dry run

      terminal
      $ npm run eval -- --fixtures private-fixtures/home --provider vision-jev --dry-run

    2. Measure

      Add OPEN_ROUTER_API_KEY to .env and run with --confirm-paid. A typical photo costs a fraction of a cent. Read report.md.

      Guide: docs/phase0.md

    Where we are

    Four phases, honest about each.

    Phase 0 Now

    Verify provider contracts, annotate real photos, measure discovery and identity against the gate.

    Phase 1 Next

    The full journey with clearly labelled Demo detections: rooms, camera, Same object?, inventory, ZIP.

    Phase 2 Later

    Live AI behind a private proxy with enforced budgets, overlay revisits and comparison statuses.

    Phase 3 Later

    Recovery, storage pressure, accessibility polish and an offline app shell.

    Out of scope on purpose: 3D mapping, continuous tracking, insurance valuation and any claim that the whole home has been inventoried.
